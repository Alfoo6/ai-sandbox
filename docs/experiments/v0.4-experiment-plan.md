# v0.4 — Controlled fitness and duration experiments

## Status and question

Proposal only: no fitness implementation, training, architecture change, or model
modification is authorized by this document. Preserve the v0.3.9 champion and
v0.3.11 artifacts. The [behavioral analysis](v0.3.11-behavior-analysis.md) motivates
testing whether an explicit collection incentive improves collection and survival;
it does not demonstrate that fitness caused the observed failures.

## Experiment 1: fitness comparison

Compare fresh, paired training runs, changing only the training fitness:

- **A — survival only:** `mean(T_i)` over the generation's training scenarios.
- **B — survival + collection:** `mean(T_i + 5 * C_i)`, where `T_i` is completed
  ticks (including the fatal tick) and `C_i` is collected food cells.

The proposed coefficient is **5 tick-equivalents per resource**, fixed before
running either arm. Six foods permit at most a 30-point explicit bonus. This is a
modest starting tradeoff, not an optimum inferred from 1027/1041. Food already
benefits survival by restoring up to 25 energy, so B deliberately adds an incentive
beyond that indirect reward; effective benefit depends on the energy cap and
collection timing. For example, B can prefer 145 ticks/2 foods (155 points) to
150 ticks/0 foods (150 points). Report the tradeoff rather than treating higher
B fitness as automatically better performance.

Do not add distance, oscillation, boundary, or per-step shaping penalties. Do not
change observation encoding, energy, food layouts, episode cap, network, mutation,
or controller behavior. If a coefficient sweep is later desired, register it as
a separate development experiment with its own budget and selection rule. Do not
adjust 5 after inspecting historical or newly reserved final-test results.

### Fixed controls and pairing

| Setting | Both arms |
| --- | --- |
| Architecture | Existing 26 → 12 tanh → 5; same encoding and action order |
| Generations | 200 (indices 0–199) |
| Population / elites | 40 / 8 |
| Training scenarios per generation | 20 |
| Mutation scale | 0.05; existing Gaussian mutation and selection algorithm |
| Evolution seeds | 42, 43, 44, 45, 46 (five paired independent initializations) |
| Scenario schedule | Existing diverse-generation sampler, schedule seed 123 |
| Candidate validation frequency | Every 10 completed generations, including 200 |
| Selection validation seeds | Proposed 300–349, subject to provenance audit |

Each A/B pair must start from identical initial parameters and use identical
scenario batches and mutation RNG streams. Parent selection will diverge as a
consequence of fitness; that is the intended treatment effect. Keep world,
controller, and evolution RNGs separate. Log exact seeds and configuration.
Shared schedule 123 conditions the result on one schedule; generalization across
training schedules would require another experiment.

The old saved champion is a historical reference only, not an initial population
or a substitute for the five A replicates. Saved `.npz` files contain inference
weights, not resumable population/RNG checkpoints.

### Data separation and model selection

1. Training uses existing disjoint generation blocks starting at seed 10000.
2. Use 300–349 only for periodic candidate selection. Before launching, audit
   existing experiment manifests to confirm these proposed seeds are unused;
   replace and freeze the range if necessary. Historical 200–249 has already
   influenced the old champion and is not the proposed selection set.
3. Reserve proposed **2000–2099** as a new final-test set, again subject to an
   unused-seed audit before freezing. It is outside training blocks and selection
   seeds. Neither range has been executed for this proposal.
4. **Never use 1000–1099 for model selection, fitness tuning, coefficient choice,
   early stopping, or new confirmatory claims.** Keep its old results as history.

At each scheduled validation event, evaluate only the current training-batch
winner, chosen by that arm's fitness; training ties retain population order.
For both arms, retain the candidate with highest **validation mean survival**,
with earliest candidate retained on ties, matching the existing criterion.
Food collection is reported but does not break ties. This keeps selection criteria
constant and asks whether food-shaped training improves a survival-selected
policy. An arm-specific composite validation criterion would test a different
combined training-and-selection intervention and is out of scope.

Retain one selected champion per run; do not select the best evolution seed for
reporting. Freeze all ten champions and the analysis before final evaluation.
Do not inspect final-test results to decide which runs, coefficient, or generations
to keep. Any result-driven redesign needs a new untouched final-test set.

### Outcomes and analysis

Primary endpoint: each selected champion's mean survival ticks on the new final
scenarios. Key secondary endpoint: mean resources collected. Also report medians,
proportion collecting any food, survival above 100 ticks, unique positions,
boundary movement fraction, blocked moves, and oscillation metrics with the
v0.3.11 definitions. Behavioral metrics are descriptive, not optimization targets.
Include random and rule-based references with controller seed = scenario seed.

For each evolution seed, compute B−A differences in mean survival and food on
identical scenarios. Report all five paired differences, their mean, sample SD,
and range. Also provide paired scenario wins/ties/losses separately for survival
and food. Do not treat 500 run-by-scenario observations as 500 independent training
replicates: the training-replicate count is five and scenarios are shared. With
five runs, avoid strong significance or robustness claims; an uncertainty interval
would require specifying a method and its assumptions before inspecting results.

A positive mean survival difference is directional evidence for the primary
question, not proof of superiority. Food improvement with survival loss is a
tradeoff, not success on the primary endpoint. Inspect replicate consistency and
magnitude. Do not rank arms by raw training fitness because their scales differ,
and do not compare generations' changing-batch training scores as fixed benchmarks.

### Budget and implementation prerequisites

Per run: `200 × 40 × 20 = 160,000` training episodes, plus
`20 × 50 = 1,000` selection-validation episodes. Ten runs total **1,610,000**
training/validation episodes, at most **805,000,000 ticks**. Final evaluation adds
1,000 neural episodes plus 200 baseline episodes. These are workload bounds, not
runtime estimates. Measure a small authorized pilot before committing resources.

Future implementation must add explicit fitness metadata and component logging,
verify A exactly reproduces the old survival computation, and test B on synthetic
results, including capped food energy. Test pairing, seed separation, unchanged
architecture, deterministic selection, and artifact overwrite protection. Record
code revision/diff, runtime versions, all seeds, model hashes, selected generation,
and both fitness components. Pilot timing must not become unrecorded tuning.
No such implementation or pilot is performed here.

## Experiment 2: longer duration, separately controlled

Test **200 vs 400 generations** with survival-only fitness A held fixed. Do not
combine longer duration and new fitness in the first comparison: their effects
would be confounded. Keep population, elites, mutation, batch size, architecture,
scenario schedule, and five evolution seeds identical to Experiment 1.

A future 400-generation run can supply paired endpoint snapshots at completed
200 and 400 generations: the schedule is prefix-stable and does not depend on the
requested run length. Verify this property in tests before use. Do not resume
from the saved champion; start from paired initial populations, or use a separately
implemented, validated full-state checkpoint if available in the future.

For the **primary duration comparison**, take the current batch winner at each
endpoint and evaluate both on the same development scenarios, without selecting
between checkpoints. This avoids giving the 400-generation treatment twice as
many opportunities to select a favorable validation champion. Optionally report
best-so-far validation champions every 10 generations as a **secondary operational
comparison**, explicitly noting the 20-vs-40 candidate-selection opportunities.
Longer training also sees more scenarios and consumes more compute; it does not
isolate extra optimization steps from extra data exposure.

Use a separate audited development range (proposed 350–399) and reserve a distinct
untouched final range (proposed 2100–2199) if Experiment 1's final results have been
examined. Freeze endpoint and metric rules before opening that final set. Never
use historical 1000–1099. If both studies are planned jointly, record all test
access and comparisons in advance rather than reusing a test set adaptively.

Five 400-generation runs cost 1,600,000 training episodes. Evaluating two endpoints
on 50 development seeds costs 500 episodes; optional periodic validation adds
10,000 episodes if run every 10 generations. Report endpoint survival and food,
paired 400−200 differences across evolution seeds, runtime, and compute cost.
Apply the same limitations concerning five replicates and a single schedule.

## Decisions still to freeze before execution

Confirm the coefficient 5 and scientific priority of survival versus food; audit
and freeze the proposed seed ranges; confirm the five-run compute budget after a
small timing pilot; and decide whether the separate duration study is affordable.
These are planning decisions, not reasons to inspect final-test outcomes. Neither
study is guaranteed to eliminate directional bias or oscillation, and a temporary
reversal after food collection should not itself be treated as failure.
